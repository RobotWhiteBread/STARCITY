Who Paid for Lincoln's City Campaigns

Mayor and City Council, 2023 and 2025 · Published November 21, 2026

Published November 21, 2026 by STARCITY, starcitylincoln.org, from the state's campaign finance files as they stood that day.

Summary

Candidates raise and spend their own money. Separately, parties, political committees and organizations can spend on their own to support or oppose a candidate. Nebraska calls that an independent expenditure, and it must be reported to the state. This paper adds up every independent expenditure the state has on file for Lincoln's mayor and City Council races in 2023 and 2025, shows what it bought, and follows each group back to its own reported funders as far as the public record goes.

  1. $1,068,502 in independent spending is on file for these races, from 8 groups.
  2. $308,249 of it was described as mail or printing, $250,076 as TV, radio or digital advertising, and $349,377 as advertising with no kind stated. The remaining $160,800 was described another way or not at all.
  3. For 3 of the 8 groups, the public record stops at the group's name. These files show what they spent and not who funded them.

This paper reports what was filed. It does not say whether any of the spending was right or wrong, and it treats every candidate and every group the same way.

Who funded the groups

A group's donors paid for everything the group did, across the state and across several years. No filing ties a particular donor to a particular mailer or ad. Read these lists as who stood behind each group, not as who paid for a specific message. Names are spelled as the state's files spell them, so one person can appear on two lines.

Who paid for the mailer in your mailbox?

  1. Find the line on the mailer that says who paid for it. Campaign material in Nebraska generally has to carry one.
  2. Search that exact name in the state's campaign filing search.
  3. If it is a candidate's committee, its donors are listed. If it is a party or political committee, its donors are listed too, as in the section above.
  4. If it is a business or nonprofit organization, the search shows what it spent and stops there. That is where disclosed money ends.

Method and limits

  • Source. Nebraska Accountability and Disclosure Commission bulk data: Contributions and Loans, and Expenditures, for 2022 through 2026, downloaded November 21, 2026. 93,711 expenditure records and 134,124 receipt records after removing repeated record IDs across the yearly files.
  • Which races. Records whose office field reads "Lincoln - Mayor" or "Lincoln - City Council", and whose type is Independent Expenditure. The office label is the state's own.
  • What it bought. Sorted by keywords in each record's description (mail, postage, print; TV, radio, digital). A record that says only "advertising" is counted as advertising with no kind stated.
  • Geography. Lincoln means a Nebraska address with a ZIP code starting 685. Donors of $250 or less are reported as a lump with no address.
  • Not checked against paper filings. Amended filings are included as the state's file holds them. Two pairs of records share the same date, amount and payee and were kept as filed.
  • What this cannot show. Spending that was never reported, spending described as education and not as support or opposition, federal races, and the donors behind organizations that are not required to list them.
  • Names. Campaign donors and filers are public record under Nebraska law. They appear here only as the state publishes them. If a record here is wrong, report it on the project's issues page and it will be checked against the state's file and corrected.
