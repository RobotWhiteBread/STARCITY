Where the City's Data Stops

And what would fill each gap · Last updated November 21, 2026

STARCITY uses only data that public agencies already publish. This page lists the places where that data stops: where it is missing, out of date, or published in a form that cannot be mapped. For each one it says what the map does in the meantime and what would fill the gap. Every item is something an agency could fix, and take credit for.

How to read this

  • A gap marked Confirmed is one we hit while building the map or its papers.
  • A gap marked To confirm is one we looked for and did not find. The data may exist somewhere we did not look. If you know where, tell us.
  • Where the map fills a gap with an estimate, the estimate is labeled as one, with its method.
  • Where a gap cannot be filled honestly, the map shows nothing. It does not show placeholders.

When money is spent on a public vote

A campaign for an office or a ballot question asks the public for something. When outside money is spent on that ask, these are the records that would let anyone follow it from the check to the result. Each line is a question a resident could answer in a minute if the record existed.

  1. Who paid. Funders of every group that spends, filed before the vote. Today three of eight groups in Lincoln's last two city elections list none.
  2. What it bought. Mail, broadcast, digital or canvassing, in set categories. Today a third of the spending is described only as advertising.
  3. Where it was aimed. Districts or ZIP codes and piece counts for each mailing or ad buy. Today this is not collected.
  4. What the vote was, precinct by precinct. The official precinct report for every election, with early votes in their home precinct, as a spreadsheet. Today it is posted for some elections and not others.
  5. Who turned out. Turnout by precinct, which the county already posts.

With all five, the map could show a dollar leaving a funder, landing as mail in a set of precincts, and the vote in those precincts beside it. With what exists today it can show the first step for five groups, the second in part, and the fourth for some elections. See the votes paper and the campaign money paper.

How to ask for it

  • Ask the agency named in the table. Most of these are a records question, not a budget question.
  • Bring the specific row. "Publish crash coordinates" is easier to say yes to than "more open data".
  • Lincoln's City Council adopted an open data resolution on August 8, 2016, committing the city to publish machine-readable data in a central place. Each row here is a way to meet it.
  • If an agency publishes one of these, report it on the project's issues page and the map will use it.

Report a filled gap or a missing row at the project's issues page. See also how the papers are checked and The STARCITY Papers.
