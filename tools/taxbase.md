# Where Lincoln's Property Value Lives

*Published November 21, 2026 by STARCITY, starcitylincoln.org. Data retrieved November 21, 2026 unless a section says otherwise.*

## Summary

Lincoln's 2026 assessment roll puts **$44.4 billion** of value on **102,700 parcels** inside the city limits. This paper asks a plain question of that roll: where is the value, and how much land does it take to hold it?

1. **Houses carry the roll.** Improved residential parcels are 85.2% of the parcels and **62.3% of the value**, $27.63 billion. Improved commercial property is 6.9% of the parcels and 35.0% of the value.
2. **Value is concentrated.** The 100 most valuable parcels hold 9.3% of all assessed value. The top 1% of parcels (1,027) hold 24.8%. Half the city's value sits on 14.8% of its parcels.
3. **A tenth of the land holds 44% of the value.** Rank every acre by the value on it, and the top 10% of parcel land holds 43.7% of the city's assessed value. The bottom half holds 2.9%.
4. **Downtown is in a class of its own.** The assessor's downtown commercial district is assessed at **$6.9 million an acre**. The large commercial districts on the east and south sides run $0.77 to $0.95 million an acre.
5. **Small lots out-produce large ones.** The fifth of houses on the smallest lots are assessed at $3.9 million an acre. The fifth on the largest lots are assessed at $1.1 million an acre.

Every figure comes from the Lancaster County Assessor's published parcel layer, measured inside Lincoln's corporate limit.

**34% of the parcel land inside the city limits is classed unimproved**, 20,486 acres, carrying 2.7% of the value. Some of that is floodplain, some is held for development, some is airport and other public land, and some is parking and yards attached to a business next door.

The median improved residential parcel is assessed at **$276,900**. The median improved commercial parcel is assessed at $702,450.

## Value per acre

Total value tells you who is big. Value per acre tells you which land is working hardest, and it is the number that matters to a city budget, because streets, pipes and fire coverage are paid for by the acre and the mile.

| Share of parcel land, ranked by value per acre | Share of assessed value held | Value per acre at the cutoff |
|---|---|---|
| Top 1% | 12.3% | $5.3 million |
| Top 5% | 29.2% | $2.6 million |
| Top 10% | 43.7% | $2.0 million |
| Top 25% | 74.1% | $1.3 million |
| Top 50% | 97.1% | $0.17 million |

Laid on an 800 metre grid, the most valuable cell in the city is the heart of downtown: **$659 million on 118 parcel acres, $5.6 million an acre**. The median cell is assessed at $0.85 million an acre. The ten most valuable cells hold 8.2% of the city's value on 2.0% of its parcel land.

## What a median home pays

The county's own TIF layer records a consolidated levy of **$1.766448 per $100** of value for the Lincoln tax district in tax year 2024. Applied to the 2026 median improved residential value of $276,900, that is about **$4,890 a year** before the homestead exemption and state property tax credits. The 2026 levy will differ. This is a yardstick, not a bill.

## What these numbers are not

- **Not the taxable base.** The layer publishes assessed value and leaves taxable value blank. Some high-value parcels are exempt from tax, such as hospitals, churches and public property that carries an assessment. This paper measures where value sits, not who pays.
- **Not the whole city by area.** Parcels inside the corporate limit total 59,524 acres. The city covers 68,296. The rest is streets, water and public land that is not on the roll as parcels.
- **Not a statement about any owner.** No owner names were used or stored. Parcels are described by class, district and size only.
- **Not a judgment about how anyone should live.** Value per acre measures what land contributes to the roll. It says nothing about whether a large yard is worth having.
- **792 parcels carry no assessed value.** They cover 3,313 acres and are counted in land totals.

## What to do with it

**If you are a realtor or appraiser.** The neighborhood table is a quick reference for where a listing sits against its association's median, and the commercial table gives a value per acre benchmark for each district.

**If you are on the council or the planning commission.** Value per acre is the missing column in most annexation and rezoning discussions. New land at the city's edge has to support the same lane-miles and pipe as older land, and the lot-size table shows how much less value per acre large lots carry.

**If you are a resident.** Your neighborhood's line in the table shows what the ground under it contributes.

## Method

Parcels are the Lancaster County Assessor's published layer with 2026 assessed values (field CNTASSDVAL), all 122,748 parcels in the county, pulled November 21, 2026. A parcel is counted as in Lincoln when a point inside it falls within the corporate limit (106.7 square miles; the larger extraterritorial jurisdiction polygon in the same layer is excluded). Areas are computed in an equal-area projection (EPSG:5070), not from a published area field. Neighborhood associations are the city's published boundaries. The scripts are in the project repository under tools/analysis.
