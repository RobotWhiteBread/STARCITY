# Lincoln and the Rest of Lancaster County

*Published November 21, 2026 by STARCITY, starcitylincoln.org. Data retrieved November 21, 2026 unless a section says otherwise.*

## Summary

The STARCITY papers so far stop at Lincoln's city limits. The county assessor's records do not. This paper uses the same parcel, sales and levy data to set Lincoln beside the rest of Lancaster County: its villages, acreages and farms.

1. **Lincoln is 84% of the county's parcels and 83% of its assessed value, on 12% of its parcel land.** 102,700 of 122,740 parcels, $44.38 billion of $53.44 billion, on 59,524 of 514,458 acres.
2. **An acre in Lincoln is assessed at about 37 times an acre outside it.** $745,517 an acre inside the city limits, $19,925 outside.
3. **Outside Lincoln, the land is farms.** 7,151 agricultural parcels cover 395,669 acres, 87% of the parcel land outside the city, and are assessed at $3.58 billion, about $9,041 an acre.
4. **Homes outside Lincoln are assessed higher.** The median improved residential parcel is $439,000 outside the city (9,748 homes) and $276,900 inside it (87,481 homes).
5. **The tax rate is lower outside Lincoln, and the typical bill is higher.** The median home outside the city sits in a tax district with a 2025 levy of 1.3755 per $100, against 1.6663 in Lincoln. Because the homes are worth more, the estimated tax on the median home is about $6,039 outside and $4,614 inside, before credits.
6. **One county, 108 tax districts.** Homes outside Lincoln fall in 97 different combinations of school, fire, village and other levies, with rates from about 1.14 to 1.59 per $100 for the middle 80% of homes.

Nebraska values agricultural land at 75% of market value for tax purposes, and homes and commercial property at 100%. That is state law, and it is one reason farm land carries a low value per acre here.

## Are homes assessed the same way inside and outside the city?

The same sales ratio test used in the assessment paper, run separately on valid residential sales from November 2025 to November 2026:

| | Sales | Median sale price | Median ratio | Coefficient of dispersion | Price-related differential |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lincoln | 3,340 | $320,000 | 0.920 | 12.6 | 1.037 |
| Rest of county | 303 | $420,000 | 0.888 | 18.6 | 1.021 |
| Whole county | 3,643 | $325,000 | 0.918 | 13.1 | 1.037 |

Outside Lincoln, assessments ran a little further below sale prices and were less consistent from one home to the next. That is common where sales are few and properties vary more: an acreage with outbuildings is harder to value than a house in a subdivision. With only 303 sales, the figures outside the city are much less certain than the city's. These are sales made after the 2026 values were set. The state's official 2026 study of the whole county, on earlier sales, found a median of 0.970, a dispersion of 4.97 and a differential of 1.006, all within the standard. See the [assessment paper](assessment.html) for why the two differ.

## Checked against outside figures

- **Value.** The Nebraska Department of Revenue lists Lancaster County's 2025 taxable value as $47.16 billion ([Table 11](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/pad/research/annual-reports/2025/annrpt2025%20table%2011.pdf)). This paper's $53.44 billion is about 13% higher. It is the 2026 roll, and it includes property that is assessed but exempt from tax. How much of the gap each explains has not been measured.
- **Tax.** The state reports $752.4 million in property taxes levied in Lancaster County for 2025 ([average rates](https://revenue.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/pad/research/valuation/2026/avgrate2025.pdf)). This paper's estimate from parcel values and district levies comes to $806,142,295, about 7% higher, for the same reasons. Treat every tax figure here as an estimate before credits and exemptions.
- **Levies.** The sixteen separate 2025 rates for Lincoln's main tax district add to 1.666284, the consolidated levy used here.

How every paper was checked is on the [verification page](verification.html).

## What these numbers are not

- **Not taxable value.** The parcel layer publishes assessed value. Exempt property is included.
- **Not tax bills.** Estimates multiply assessed value by the district levy. They leave out the state school tax credit, homestead exemptions and special valuation.
- **Not about any owner.** No owner names or addresses were used.
- **Not the villages one by one.** This paper splits the county only at Lincoln's corporate limit. Waverly, Hickman, Bennet and the other towns are inside "rest of county".

## Method

Parcels are the Lancaster County Assessor's published layer, 122,740 parcels with geometry, retrieved November 21, 2026. A parcel is counted as in Lincoln when a point inside it falls within the corporate limit, the second-largest shape in the city's limits layer. Areas are computed in an equal-area projection. Levies are each tax district's 2025 consolidated rate from the Nebraska Department of Revenue. Sales are the county's file of valid residential sales, validity code 01.
